applicant, and also my own counselor

I know it's hard. I just did all of it.

Hi, I'm Katherine, or Gia Han if you're from home. I'm a senior at a public high school in Ho Chi Minh City, and my school has never had a college counselor. When I started applying to the US, I thought the hard part would be the essay. Then I opened the Common App, saw the box asking for my counselor, and slowly realized the person handling my school's half of the application was going to be, more or less, me.

So I made my school's first school profile. I made a transcript template so a 9.6 on a 10-point scale would make sense to an admissions officer in Massachusetts. I asked my homeroom teacher to be my counselor, and we figured out the forms together, one confused email at a time. I did the CSS Profile, wrote to financial aid offices about my family's situation, and started building my own little app to track every school, because a spreadsheet just wasn't enough anymore (it's still not finished, honestly). Somewhere in between, I wrote my essays.

I got through it, and I got into NYU Abu Dhabi. I also made rookie mistakes that someone who had done this once would have caught in five minutes, like waiting until right before a deadline to start things, uploading the wrong file, getting a format wrong, and picking schools for their brand names instead of what my family could actually pay and what I actually wanted to study. That's the whole reason this exists. You shouldn't have to learn everything the slow way like I did, and you shouldn't need agency prices to skip it.
