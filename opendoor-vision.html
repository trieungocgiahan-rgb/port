The broken system

Today, the guidance a student gets depends on what their family can pay.

With money
$15,000–20,000

What a premium consulting firm such as Crimson charges a Vietnamese family for admissions support.

Without it
0 counselors

What most public school students have. No one at school to explain the process, the essays or the aid.

The deeper failure

Knowledge evaporates.

Every year hundreds of thousands of students learn this whole process the hard way. They get in, they leave, and what they learned leaves with them. The next class starts from zero again. No one has built the system that catches this knowledge and hands it forward.

Each class starts near empty. What it learns drains away when it graduates.

How it stays free

The bigger Crimson gets, the more it costs. The bigger OpenDoor gets, the cheaper it gets.

A consulting firm has to hire an expert for every new family. OpenDoor's experts are last year's students, and a guide written once can be read by thousands.

The loop that pays for it

1 · UseStudents use the free guides and mentors
2 · Get inThey get into schools abroad
3 · Come backThey answer, review essays and update guides
4 · ImproveNext year's version is better than ours

And money?

We don't need to make money to start. Year one runs on free tools and our own time, so the only costs are a domain, hosting and some AI usage. Later, money only has to cover running costs and pay the people who keep OpenDoor going. We'll pick a model once students are actually using it.

The guides, the Library and the community stay free, forever.

  • Optional 1:1 mentor sessionsLaterFor students who want more time. Most of the fee goes to the mentor.
  • Licenses for schools with no counselorLaterTeacher dashboards that use the same free content.
  • Sponsors and foundationsLaterFunding scholarship spots and paying for AI Review for students who can't.
  • University info sessionsLaterOpt-in only. No one can ever pay to influence an admission decision.

First steps

Start small. Here's the next 12 weeks.

We start with what we already have: what we learned from applying. Each phase ends with a check we can actually measure.

W1W2W3W4W5W6W7W8W9W10W11W12
Weeks 1–2

Write down what we know

  • Each of us writes our own application story and the 3 things we wish we'd known
  • Pick the first 10 guides
  • Anonymize our own profiles as the first Profile Bank entries

Done when10 guide outlines and 5 profiles exist.

Weeks 3–6

Ship a simple website

  • The Library with the first 10 guides, in Vietnamese and English
  • A shared deadline calendar and a school list template
  • An "Ask a question" form that comes straight to us
  • Host it on a free tier. No login needed to read.

Done whenThe site is live and a friend finds the guide they need in under a minute.

Weeks 7–9

Bring back the people who got in

  • Invite 15–20 friends and seniors who got in as founding mentors
  • Each mentor answers questions and updates one guide
  • Test AI Review on 10 real essays, with a mentor checking every comment

Done whenEvery question gets a real answer within 48 hours.

Weeks 10–12

Find the first 500 students

  • Share in school group chats, Threads and TikTok, starting with our own schools
  • Watch 20 students at schools with no counselor use the site
  • Turn their most common questions into the next 10 guides

Done when500 students opened a guide and 50 asked a question.
